Effective Improvement in Delta, BC, demands a thorough understanding of the region's complex subsurface conditions, predominantly composed of compressible marine silts, soft clays, and peat deposits overlying dense glacial till. These fine-grained soils, characteristic of the Fraser River delta, present significant challenges for construction, including high settlement potential, low bearing capacity, and liquefaction susceptibility under seismic loading. A robust geotechnical program, starting with a comprehensive investigation, is essential to characterize these variable strata and assess their response to treatment. This data directly informs the selection of suitable techniques, ensuring compliance with the British Columbia Building Code and local municipal requirements for structural integrity and long-term performance.

The selection and design of a Improvement methodology rely entirely on accurate in-situ data obtained through standardized Canadian testing protocols. The Cone Penetration Test (CPT) provides near-continuous profiling of soil behaviour type, stratigraphy, and pore pressure dissipation, which is critical for evaluating consolidation characteristics in soft clays and identifying liquefiable layers. This is often supplemented by Standard Penetration Test (SPT) borings to recover disturbed samples and correlate with existing empirical design methods. To directly assess the undrained shear strength of sensitive deltaic clays, the field vane shear test (VST) is an indispensable tool, providing a direct measurement crucial for embankment stability analysis and staged construction planning.

Typical projects in Delta, from highway embankments along Highway 17 to industrial warehousing and port infrastructure, frequently require advanced ground treatment to mitigate total and differential settlement. Our In-Situ programs are designed to provide the critical parameters for designing preloading with vertical drains, deep soil mixing, or rigid inclusions. For granular fill placement and compaction control, the field density test (sand cone method) is routinely employed to verify that specified relative compaction levels are achieved, ensuring the performance of engineered fills and working platforms. The data generated directly supports the numerical modeling required to predict post-construction settlement magnitudes and rates.

Our process integrates field data, laboratory testing, and rigorous analysis to deliver clear, actionable recommendations. Following the initial subsurface exploration, we perform settlement and stability analyses calibrated to the site-specific ground model. The final engineering report provides a detailed Improvement design, including treatment depths, spacing, and performance specifications, along with a quality control testing plan. This comprehensive approach allows contractors to proceed with confidence, transforming challenging Delta soils into reliable, buildable ground while effectively managing project risk and long-term maintenance liabilities.
